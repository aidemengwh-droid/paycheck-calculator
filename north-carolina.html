How North Carolina Income Tax Works

North Carolina uses a flat income tax rate of 4.2%, meaning all residents pay the same percentage regardless of income level.

The state offers a standard deduction of $12,750, which reduces your taxable income before the 4.2% rate is applied. For example, a single filer earning $60,000 would pay state income tax on $47,250 of income, resulting in a state tax bill of approximately $2,008.

Compared to states with progressive tax systems, North Carolina's flat rate means lower-income workers pay the same percentage as high earners. This can be advantageous for high-income earners but may result in a higher effective tax rate for those with modest incomes.

Federal Taxes for North Carolina Residents

All North Carolina residents pay federal income tax using the same 2026 federal tax brackets, regardless of which state they live in. The federal standard deduction for 2026 is $16,100 for single filers, $32,200 for married filing jointly, and $24,150 for head of household. Federal tax rates range from 10% to 37% across seven brackets.

In addition to federal income tax, all workers pay FICA taxes: Social Security at 6.2% (capped at $184,500 in 2026) and Medicare at 1.45%. High earners also pay an additional 0.9% Medicare surtax on wages above $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married). These federal taxes are the same no matter which state you live in.

How North Carolina Compares to Other States

With a top state income tax rate of 4.2%, North Carolina falls in the middle range among U.S. states. This is lower than high-tax states like California (up to 13.3%) and New York (up to 10.9%), but higher than no-tax states like Texas, Florida, and Washington. The moderate rate provides a balance between funding state services and keeping take-home pay reasonable.

Tips for North Carolina Taxpayers

To maximize your take-home pay in North Carolina, consider the following strategies:

  • Adjust your W-4 withholdings to match your actual tax liability. Over-withholding gives the government an interest-free loan; under-withholding can result in penalties.
  • Take advantage of pre-tax deductions like 401(k) contributions, Health Savings Accounts (HSAs), and Flexible Spending Accounts (FSAs). These reduce both your federal and North Carolina state taxable income.
  • Review your filing status annually. Marriage, divorce, or having dependents can significantly impact your tax situation.
  • Track deductible expenses throughout the year, especially if you plan to itemize deductions instead of taking the standard deduction.

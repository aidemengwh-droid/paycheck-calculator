How Wisconsin Income Tax Works

Wisconsin uses a progressive income tax system with 4 tax brackets, ranging from 3.5% to 7.6%. Wisconsin has a progressive income tax with four brackets ranging from 3.54% to 7.65%. The state offers a substantial standard deduction of $12,880, which significantly reduces taxable income for most filers.

The state provides a standard deduction of $12,880, which lowers your taxable income before the progressive rates are applied. This means your effective state tax rate will always be lower than the top marginal rate, even for high earners.

With a progressive system, your income is taxed in portions—each portion falls into a specific bracket and is taxed at that bracket's rate. For example, if you earn $75,000 in Wisconsin, the first portion of your income is taxed at 3.5%, and only the amount exceeding each bracket threshold is taxed at the higher rates. Your effective (average) tax rate will be lower than the top marginal rate of 7.6%.

Federal Taxes for Wisconsin Residents

All Wisconsin residents pay federal income tax using the same 2026 federal tax brackets, regardless of which state they live in. The federal standard deduction for 2026 is $16,100 for single filers, $32,200 for married filing jointly, and $24,150 for head of household. Federal tax rates range from 10% to 37% across seven brackets.

In addition to federal income tax, all workers pay FICA taxes: Social Security at 6.2% (capped at $184,500 in 2026) and Medicare at 1.45%. High earners also pay an additional 0.9% Medicare surtax on wages above $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married). These federal taxes are the same no matter which state you live in.

How Wisconsin Compares to Other States

With a top state income tax rate of 7.6%, Wisconsin ranks among the higher-tax states in the country. This is comparable to states like New York (up to 10.9%) and Hawaii (up to 10%), and significantly higher than no-tax states like Texas, Florida, and Nevada. High earners in Wisconsin should carefully consider the impact of state taxes on their overall financial planning.

Tips for Wisconsin Taxpayers

To maximize your take-home pay in Wisconsin, consider the following strategies:

  • Adjust your W-4 withholdings to match your actual tax liability. Over-withholding gives the government an interest-free loan; under-withholding can result in penalties.
  • Take advantage of pre-tax deductions like 401(k) contributions, Health Savings Accounts (HSAs), and Flexible Spending Accounts (FSAs). These reduce both your federal and Wisconsin state taxable income.
  • Review your filing status annually. Marriage, divorce, or having dependents can significantly impact your tax situation.
  • Track deductible expenses throughout the year, especially if you plan to itemize deductions instead of taking the standard deduction.

How Kentucky Income Tax Works

Kentucky uses a flat income tax rate of 4%, meaning all residents pay the same percentage regardless of income level. Kentucky applies a flat 4% income tax rate to all residents. The state has been moving toward tax simplification in recent years.

Since Kentucky does not offer a state-level standard deduction, your full taxable income (after federal adjustments) is subject to the 4% rate. For a worker earning $60,000, the state income tax would be approximately $2,400 per year.

Compared to states with progressive tax systems, Kentucky's flat rate means lower-income workers pay the same percentage as high earners. This can be advantageous for high-income earners but may result in a higher effective tax rate for those with modest incomes.

Federal Taxes for Kentucky Residents

All Kentucky residents pay federal income tax using the same 2026 federal tax brackets, regardless of which state they live in. The federal standard deduction for 2026 is $16,100 for single filers, $32,200 for married filing jointly, and $24,150 for head of household. Federal tax rates range from 10% to 37% across seven brackets.

In addition to federal income tax, all workers pay FICA taxes: Social Security at 6.2% (capped at $184,500 in 2026) and Medicare at 1.45%. High earners also pay an additional 0.9% Medicare surtax on wages above $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married). These federal taxes are the same no matter which state you live in.

How Kentucky Compares to Other States

With a top state income tax rate of 4%, Kentucky falls in the middle range among U.S. states. This is lower than high-tax states like California (up to 13.3%) and New York (up to 10.9%), but higher than no-tax states like Texas, Florida, and Washington. The moderate rate provides a balance between funding state services and keeping take-home pay reasonable.

Tips for Kentucky Taxpayers

To maximize your take-home pay in Kentucky, consider the following strategies:

  • Adjust your W-4 withholdings to match your actual tax liability. Over-withholding gives the government an interest-free loan; under-withholding can result in penalties.
  • Take advantage of pre-tax deductions like 401(k) contributions, Health Savings Accounts (HSAs), and Flexible Spending Accounts (FSAs). These reduce both your federal and Kentucky state taxable income.
  • Review your filing status annually. Marriage, divorce, or having dependents can significantly impact your tax situation.
  • Track deductible expenses throughout the year, especially if you plan to itemize deductions instead of taking the standard deduction.
